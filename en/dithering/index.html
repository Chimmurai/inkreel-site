Dithering: fitting tones into two colors.

Updated: November 22, 2026

In a 1-bit image every pixel is either ink or paper. Dithering breaks the grays into dots and strokes of those two colors, and the eye puts them back together as a halftone.

Why tones in two colors

That is how newspapers were printed: a photograph broken into a screen of dots of different sizes. It is how devices that only know on and off work: the first Macintosh, receipt thermal printers, e-paper screens, the Playdate’s black-and-white display.

Two colors are the most compact and consistent form of a picture, and dithering is the way not to lose light and shadow in it.

One frame, five ways

Here is the same 192 × 192 pixel frame five times. The first four are classic algorithms written the textbook way; the fifth picture is InkReel at the Engraving level.

Four classic methods

Threshold.
Each pixel is compared with a single limit. The fastest, gives crisp lines and text, but the tones vanish: gray becomes either black or white.
Ordered dithering (the Bayer matrix, 1973).
The limit changes along a repeating pattern, so gray becomes a regular grid of dots. Predictable and stable in video, but the pattern shows.
Error diffusion (Floyd and Steinberg, 1976).
What was lost on one pixel is added to its neighbors. It gives a fine, natural texture, but in motion the dots can “boil”.
Atkinson (Bill Atkinson, the first Macintosh).
It spreads only three quarters of the error, so the darkest and lightest places stay clean and the contrast is higher.

Why one algorithm is not enough

Sky, fur, water, text and a smooth wall ask for different things. A regular grid suits the sky but spoils text; strokes look good on fur and leave litter on a smooth surface.

So InkReel does not pick one method for the whole frame: it looks at what is in each area and chooses a threshold, a blue-noise halftone, or strokes that follow the form. The five quality levels are a path from a quick Sketch to a detailed Engraving. The same frame always gives the same pixels.

Dithering in video

In motion every method has one more requirement: the picture must not flicker. Error diffusion scatters the dots anew on every frame, and the scene “boils”. InkReel keeps the pattern stable from frame to frame, so at the Engraving level video does not flicker.

In short

How is dithering different from ordinary black and white?

In ordinary black and white (grayscale) every pixel has hundreds of brightness levels. In 1-bit there are only two, and the halftones come from how the pixels are arranged.

Can I make pixel art from this?

Yes. Set a small output size (128–192 px on the long side) and the pixels become large. There are examples in the gallery.
